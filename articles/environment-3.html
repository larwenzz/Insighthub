Plastics: Is It Really Necessary?

In the modern world, plastics have become an important tool in our everyday lives. They are used for food packaging, containers, household products, electronics, transportation, and many other things because they are lightweight, durable, and relatively inexpensive. However, the convenience that plastics provide also comes with environmental costs. Most plastics are produced from fossil fuels, and their production, use, and disposal contribute to pollution and greenhouse gas emissions.

Plastic itself is not always harmful when it is used responsibly. It can provide benefits in many areas of society, including food preservation, medicine, construction, and transportation. The problem begins when plastic is used unnecessarily or treated as something that can simply be thrown away after one use.

According to the United Nations Environment Programme, addressing plastic pollution requires more than simply cleaning up waste; it also requires reducing unnecessary plastic use, encouraging reuse, and improving recycling systems.

One of the biggest problems is the amount of plastic waste produced every day. Packaging is one of the largest sources of short-lived plastic products, many of which are used for only a short period before being discarded. The OECD reported that global plastic waste reached 353 million tonnes in 2019, while only about 9% was ultimately recycled (OECD, 2022). When plastic waste is poorly managed, it can enter rivers, oceans, soil, and other natural environments. Because plastic does not easily biodegrade, it can remain in the environment for long periods and gradually break into smaller particles known as microplastics (UNEP, 2023).

Plastic pollution also affects more than just the appearance of our surroundings. It can harm wildlife and ecosystems when animals encounter or consume plastic waste. Plastic pollution can also contribute to climate change because the production of plastic depends heavily on fossil fuels. The OECD estimated that the plastics lifecycle was responsible for about 3.4% of global greenhouse gas emissions in 2019, with most of those emissions coming from the production and conversion of plastics (OECD, 2022). This shows that the plastic problem is connected not only to waste management but also to the larger issue of environmental sustainability.

However, solving plastic pollution does not mean that society must completely eliminate every plastic product. Instead, we should become more thoughtful about how plastic is designed, produced, used, and disposed of. Reusable containers, refillable products, proper waste segregation, and recycling can reduce the amount of plastic that ends up in the environment. Businesses can also help by creating products that are easier to reuse or recycle, while governments can establish policies that encourage responsible production and waste management. UNEP (2023) emphasizes the importance of moving toward a circular economy in which products and materials remain useful for as long as possible instead of following a simple “take, make, and dispose” pattern.

Students and young people can also contribute to reducing plastic pollution. Simple actions such as bringing reusable bottles and bags, avoiding unnecessary single-use products, properly sorting waste, and encouraging others to do the same can become meaningful when practiced consistently. These actions may seem small compared with the global scale of plastic pollution, but they can help create better habits and greater awareness within schools, families, and communities.

Ultimately, the question is not whether plastic should completely disappear from our lives, but whether we are using it responsibly. Plastic has useful purposes, but convenience should not become an excuse for excessive consumption and careless disposal. By reducing unnecessary plastic, reusing products whenever possible, improving recycling, and supporting better environmental practices, society can enjoy the useful qualities of plastic while reducing its damage to nature. The environment does not need us to stop using every modern invention; it needs us to become more responsible with the resources we already have.

The Role of Literature in Shaping Environmental Consciousness

The article explains how literature can help people understand and care about the environment. Stories, poems, and other forms of writing can make environmental problems feel more personal and understandable, encouraging readers to think about their responsibility toward nature.

A major concept in the article is ecocriticism. Ecocriticism is the study of the relationship between literature and the natural environment. Instead of looking at nature as merely a background for a story, ecocriticism asks how nature, animals, ecosystems, and environmental problems are represented in literature.

Literature is not only a way of telling stories about nature, it can also educate people, challenge harmful attitudes toward the environment, and encourage them to protect the planet.

Environment

Flowers can represent the environment because they are part of nature and depend on a healthy ecosystem to survive. Their roots can symbolize our connection to the Earth, while different flowers can represent biodiversity. A healthy, blooming garden can show what happens when we properly care for and protect our environment.
